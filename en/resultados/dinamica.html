2006–2017 dynamics by municipal type

Results from the project’s Article C, recalculated using the Manuscript 1 types

Change in the number, area and mean area of agricultural establishments between the 2006 and 2017 Censuses, by the five municipal types from Manuscript 1 and by biome
Preliminary version for the project team. Results may change as the study progresses. English translation under review. If the two versions differ, the Portuguese text prevails.

Context and objective

The project’s Article C compared the 2006 and 2017 Agricultural Censuses, both conducted by the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE). This page recalculates those results using the five types from Manuscript 1 (provisional labels) and the dominant biome from the IBGE 1:250,000 map. The aim is to examine how the number, area and mean area of establishments changed in each group.

Data and methods

The figures refer to all agricultural establishments, not only family farms, in the 141 municipalities of Mato Grosso (MT). Intercensal changes are calculated from the municipal sum for each group. Differences between types and between biomes are assessed with the Kruskal-Wallis test on municipal percentage changes, with no correction for spatial autocorrelation. The municipal sum for the first Census does not reconcile with the state table, as detailed in the box below and in Table 7 in the appendix to this page.

The 2006 municipal sum does not reconcile with the state table

  • The sum across the 141 municipalities comes to 112,987 establishments and 48.69 million ha in 2006. The 2006–2017 state comparison table records 112,978 establishments and 47.81 million ha, a difference in area of 883 thousand ha (1.8%).
  • The IBGE Census comparison table (historical series) records 112,987 establishments and 48,688,710 ha in 2006, figures that match the municipal sum. It is not yet clear why these figures differ from the state table.
  • In 2017, the sum reconciles: 118,679 establishments and 54,922,849 ha, against 54,922,850 ha in the state total (a rounding difference).
  • The changes shown below use the municipal sum in both years, so that 2006 and 2017 share the same basis.

Value of production: excluded from this version

The 2006 value of production does not reconcile. The state table records R$9.60 billion, but the sum from the corrected municipal database (IBGE, table 1118) comes to R$12.29 billion, 28% more (2006 values at current prices). Until the difference is explained, the change in the value of production between 2006 and 2017 remains excluded from the website.

Change by type

As Table 1 and Figure 1 show, T5 recorded the largest increase in the number of establishments (22.8%), with mean area almost unchanged (−0.3%). T4, by contrast, lost 8.6% of its establishments, while its mean area increased by 20.1%, the largest rise of any type. T3 also lost establishments (−1.3%) and gained area (16.9%).

These differences appear in the sums, but the statistical evidence for them across municipalities is not clear. Table 2 complements the sums with the median municipal change. Table 3 indicates that none of the three measures differs between types in the Kruskal-Wallis test on municipal changes (p ≥ 0.19).

Change by biome

Table 5 gives the median municipal change and the tests for differences between biomes.

As Table 4 shows, the number of establishments in the Pantanal rose by 37.8%, although the biome has only 4 municipalities. In the Amazon, the number of establishments remained almost unchanged (0.6%), while area grew by 14.4%.

Concluding remarks

This page has recalculated the dynamics of agricultural establishments between the two Censuses using the five municipal types and the dominant biome. The sums indicate an increase in the number of establishments in T5, with mean area almost unchanged. They also indicate a decrease in that number in T4 and T3, with mean area rising in T4 and area rising in T3. The statistical evidence for these differences across municipalities, however, is not clear.

These results have limitations. The figures cover all agricultural establishments, not only family farms, and the municipal sum for the first Census does not reconcile with the state table. The value of production also remains excluded from this version until the difference is explained.
