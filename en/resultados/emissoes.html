Emissions and deforestation by municipal type

Results from the project’s Articles A and B, recalculated using the Manuscript 1 types

SEEG 13 emissions and PRODES deforestation across the five municipal types from Manuscript 1. The page also validates the deforestation proxy against official PRODES data and shows the PRODES series for Mato Grosso.
Preliminary version for the project team. Results may change as the study progresses. English translation under review. If the two versions differ, the Portuguese text prevails.

Context and objective

The project’s Articles A and B related an earlier typology of family farming to deforestation and to municipal emissions estimated by the Greenhouse Gas Emissions and Removals Estimation System (SEEG). This page recalculates those results using the five types from Manuscript 1 (provisional labels). It also reports the validation of the articles’ deforestation proxy against the official series from PRODES, the satellite-based deforestation monitoring system run by the National Institute for Space Research (INPE). This validation was corrected on 19 October 2026.

SEEG measures the entire municipality, including agriculture and livestock operations of all sizes, deforestation, energy, industry and urban waste. The figures below therefore describe the municipalities of each type, not family farming.

Data and methods

In the tables and tests by type and by biome, emissions come from SEEG municipal data (Observatório do Clima) and are annual means from 2010 to 2020, the period used in Article B. The time series and the column for the most recent year are shown separately. Gross emissions are the sum of the five SEEG sectors, excluding removals. They are expressed in carbon dioxide equivalent using the global warming potentials from the Fifth Assessment Report of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (GWP-AR5). Deforestation is the official PRODES increment by municipality from 2017 to 2025. For these nine years, all three PRODES biomes have consolidated annual data, and each municipality’s total is the sum of the Amazon, Cerrado and Pantanal masks.

Articles A and B estimated municipal deforestation using a proxy: SEEG land-use change emissions divided by 150 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (tCO2e) per hectare. This factor is an assumption made in the articles, has no primary source and is under review. To assess the proxy, this estimate was compared with the official deforestation rate for Mato Grosso (MT) from PRODES (Legal Amazon, forest formations). Differences between types and between biomes are assessed with the Kruskal-Wallis test, with epsilon squared (ε²) as the effect size and no correction for spatial autocorrelation.

Emissions by type

Table 1 shows gross emissions by type. Together, the 141 municipalities emit an average of 243.2 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (MtCO2e) per year. The SEEG state total for the same period is 243.4 MtCO2e per year, and the difference (0.19) is not allocated to any municipality.

The types differ in gross emissions (Kruskal-Wallis: H = 38.95; p < 0.001; ε² = 0.28). This difference tracks aggregate scale: T3, the type with the highest aggregate scale, has the highest municipal median (3.03 MtCO2e per year) and, with 20 municipalities, accounts for 27.3% of total municipal emissions. T1, with 74 municipalities, accounts for 33.7%.

Sectoral composition

Figure 1 shows that land-use change and forestry is the largest sector in every type, with a share ranging from 58.4% (T5) to 68.4% (T3). T5, which includes Cuiabá, Rondonópolis and Sinop, has the highest energy share (10.1%). Emissions from agriculture and livestock differ most between types (ε² = 0.33), as the tests in Table 2 show.

Article B divided municipal emissions by the number of family farms and by family-farm area. SEEG measures the entire municipality, so these ratios do not represent the emission intensity of family farming and serve only as context. Time series by type

Figure 2 shows the time series of gross emissions by type. It starts in 1990 because SEEG estimates land-use change and forestry emissions only from that year onwards. Gross emissions for earlier years exclude the largest sector in Mato Grosso and are therefore not comparable with those for later years.

Emissions by biome

Table 4 shows gross emissions and their sectoral composition by dominant biome, and Table 5 gives the tests for differences between biomes.

The Amazon accounts for 70.2% of total municipal emissions, and land-use change makes up 73.3% of that biome’s total. In the 4 Pantanal municipalities, by contrast, agriculture and livestock is the largest sector (56.1%).

Deforestation by type: Article A recalculated with PRODES data

Article A compared deforestation across municipal types using the SEEG proxy (2010–2020) and the earlier typology. Because the proxy lies far above PRODES, as the validation further down this page shows, this section revisits the question using the official PRODES increment by municipality and the five types from Manuscript 1.

PRODES measures the entire municipality, not family farming. The figures therefore describe the municipalities of each type.

As Table 6 shows, the 141 municipalities recorded a total of 22,969 km² of deforestation from 2017 to 2025, with T1 (74 municipalities) accounting for 34.5%. The types differ little, however: the difference is weak for total area deforested (Kruskal-Wallis: H = 9.68; p = 0.046; ε² = 0.07) and not significant for the percentage of territory deforested (p = 0.234).

As in Article A, deforestation does not track the share of family farms in category 46504 of the 2017 Census. Across the 141 municipalities, Spearman’s ρ is 0.05 (p = 0.522) with total area deforested and 0.04 (p = 0.666) with the percentage of territory deforested (Table 7).

Validation of the deforestation proxy

Correction note (19 October 2026)

  • The PRODES series previously used in the project contained Amazon data entered by hand and labelled as the official series. In every year from 2005 to 2024, this series lay above the official rate for Mato Grosso.
  • That series was replaced by the official INPE series, retrieved from TerraBrasilis on 19 October 2026. The new series contains the annual rate for Mato Grosso in the Legal Amazon and the annual increment by biome. The website does not use the old series.
  • This replacement changes the validation in Article B. For the 2010–2020 state total, the proxy is about 8 times the PRODES figure (previously “2.6 times”). The statement about “weak interannual agreement” no longer holds, because the Pearson correlation with the official rate is 0.65.
  • The text of Article B has not yet been rewritten, but the figures in this section already use the official series.

Discussion

The results indicate that in the Amazon the proxy shows close agreement with interannual variation (Spearman’s ρ = 0.86), although its level is 6.2 times that of PRODES. In the Cerrado, its level is 2.0 times that of PRODES and agreement on the ordering of the years is weak (Spearman’s ρ = 0.38). In the Pantanal, there is no agreement. SEEG counts more than forest clear-cutting, and the factor of 150 tCO2e/ha has no source. The proxy therefore serves as a relative indicator for comparing municipalities and types, not as a measure of deforested area.

The proxy was validated for the state total and by biome, but the municipality-by-municipality comparison has not yet been made. PRODES data by municipality (2017–2025) are in the section on Article A above and on the SO1 page.

Concluding remarks

This page has recalculated the results of Articles A and B on emissions and deforestation using the five municipal types. The types differ in gross emissions, in line with their aggregate scale, and land-use change and forestry is the largest sector in all of them. Deforestation measured by PRODES, by contrast, differs little between types and does not track the share of family farms in the census category of tenure awaiting title. The validation indicates that the articles’ proxy lies far above PRODES and should be treated only as a relative indicator.

These results have limitations. SEEG and PRODES measure the entire municipality, not family farming, and the tests do not correct for spatial autocorrelation. The proxy has not yet been validated municipality by municipality, and the text of Article B has yet to be rewritten using the official PRODES series.
