Project overview

RESA Project no. 115, Economic Viability of Rural Settlements in the Three Biomes of Mato Grosso: An Integrated Analysis of Sustainability, Carbon and Territorial Dynamics, is implemented by Mato Grosso State University (UNEMAT) with support from Instituto Clima e Sociedade (iCS) under Grant D-25-02928.

The central research question is whether rural settlements in Mato Grosso can be economically viable while keeping the forest standing. To answer it, the project takes an integrated view of territory, production, income and carbon. The planned end product is a Technology Package of tools, methods, models and recommendations for public policy.

The territory and geospatial technologies workstream covers geodesy, territorial and landscape analysis, secondary data, processing and the database. The socioeconomics and bioeconomy workstream covers field data collection, socioeconomic and bioeconomic analysis, valuation, scenario modelling, engagement, dissemination and database architecture. The carbon and environment workstream covers the carbon balance, environmental sustainability, forest ecology, biomass and carbon stocks, as well as scenario building and technical recommendations. These three workstreams, defined in the project proposal, contribute to the Technology Package.

Implementation phases

The project runs in three phases. Phase 1, assessment and evidence generation (SO1, SO2 and SO3), gathers information on territory, production, income and carbon from secondary and field data. Phase 2, strategies and viability modelling (SO4), builds low-carbon scenarios and carries out the financial analysis. Phase 3, Technology Package and engagement for implementation (SO5 and SO6), holds workshops with the settled families, consolidates the package and prepares a proposal for scaling up.

Research scope

The spatial scope of the research is the 141 municipalities of Mato Grosso covered by the 2017 Agricultural Census. They form the state-wide baseline and are analysed using public secondary data. The three biomes provide the basis for comparison, with each municipality assigned to the biome that occupies the largest part of its area. The assignment uses the 1:250,000 map (2019) from the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) and places 76 municipalities in the Amazon, 61 in the Cerrado and 4 in the Pantanal.

The field study has three pilot areas, one per biome: Vale do Arinos (Juara); Quatá and N. Sra. Aparecida (Lucas do Rio Verde); and Lambari (Poconé). They are described in Pilot areas. However, a municipality is not the same as a pilot area: municipal indicators provide context for the pilot areas but do not measure them.

Website development

The website is built with Quarto and R. The data are prepared in Python from the project databases, which were frozen on 19 October 2026. The figures come from public sources and from the project’s own results, such as the Manuscript 1 typology. The public sources are IBGE, the National Institute for Colonisation and Agrarian Reform (INCRA), Observatório do Clima’s Greenhouse Gas Emissions and Removals Estimation System (SEEG) and the National Institute for Space Research (INPE). Each figure is shown with its source, unit and year, and Data gives details of sources and licences.

This is a preliminary version for the project team. It will be published on GitHub Pages once the project coordination team has approved it. All pages ask search engines not to index them, and the website has no sitemap for search engines.

The website has an English version, available via the flags in the menu bar. It is a translation of the Portuguese original and is still being reviewed by the team. If the versions differ, the Portuguese text prevails.

Content rules

The website follows a set of content rules. The IBGE symbols X (suppressed), – (true zero) and … (not available) appear as published, and none of them is converted to zero or to an empty cell. Tables and files therefore show the symbol itself in the cell, and in files the sinal_<variable> column repeats it. On the map, X is shown in grey. For tenure, the 2017 Census category is always referred to as “grantee or settler awaiting a definitive title (2017 Census)”. This category is not equivalent to the total number of agrarian-reform settlers.

For the typology, the website uses only the municipal typology from Manuscript 1 (k = 5), and the type names remain provisional labels until the authors approve them. Throughout the website, the dominant biome is taken from the IBGE 1:250,000 map (2019). For deforestation, the only series used is the official one from PRODES, INPE’s satellite-based deforestation monitoring system. The series is available on TerraBrasilis and is shown with the correction note. The pilot areas appear with identifying details only, because the figures for families and area will be added once the sources have been reconciled.

The following are not published on the website: personal data of producers and beneficiaries (names, CPF/CNPJ and property-level environmental registry data), photographs of people, commercial databases with restricted licences, the full texts of manuscripts and articles, the budget, field data, photographs, and data from drones and LiDAR. For the project team, only names, roles and workstreams are published. For the literature, only metadata (authors, year, title, journal and DOI) and search counts are published, with no third-party abstracts.
