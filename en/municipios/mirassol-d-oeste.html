Mirassol d’Oeste

Profile of the municipality of Mirassol d’Oeste (MT). Its dominant biome is the Amazon, and Manuscript 1 classifies it as type T5 (provisional label). Indicators cover family farming and tenure in the 2017 Census, SIPRA and SEEG data, and 2006–2017 dynamics.
Preliminary version for the project team. The data come from the 2017 Agricultural Census (IBGE), INCRA/SIPRA (as at 3 June 2025), SEEG 13 (1970–2024) and the 2006 and 2017 Agricultural Censuses. Each value is shown with its reference year. English translation under review. If the two versions differ, the Portuguese text prevails.

Amazon T5 · Horticultural-irrigated provisional label IBGE code 5105622

Amazon 96.6% · Pantanal 3.4% of the territory (IBGE, Biomes 1:250,000, 2019). The municipality is grouped by its dominant biome.

RESA Project Manuscript 1 assigns the municipality to type T5, a group of 12 municipalities, in its municipal typology of family farming. The typology applies k-means, with k = 5, to scores from a factor analysis of 21 variables in the 2017 Agricultural Census. Typology details.

The 2017 Agricultural Census of the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) recorded 1,129 family farms in Mirassol d'Oeste, or 79.3% of the municipality’s agricultural establishments (68.8% for Mato Grosso as a whole). They occupied 39,192 ha, or 40.2% of the area of agricultural establishments, averaging 34.7 ha per establishment (municipal median in the Amazon biome: 69.8 ha; Mato Grosso mean: 62.9 ha). Persons employed on family farms totalled 2,392. The value of family-farm production was R$46.4 million (2017 values), equivalent to R$41.1 thousand per establishment. Animal production accounted for 80.4% of this value.

In 2017, 11.7% of family farms had a tractor, 7.7% received technical guidance and 15.3% obtained financing (municipal medians in the Amazon biome: 16.6%, 12.6% and 17.7%). In addition, 1.3% of family farms produced mainly for their own consumption and 39.9% produced cow’s milk.

On land tenure, the 2017 Census recorded 405 family farms in category 46504 (status of grantee or settler awaiting a definitive title), or 35.9% of the municipality’s family farms (10.8% for Mato Grosso as a whole). The Agrarian Reform Projects Information System (SIPRA) of the National Institute for Colonisation and Agrarian Reform (INCRA) records 7 settlement projects based in the municipality, with 900 settled families and 27,180 ha (Report 0227, as at 3 June 2025). Each project is counted only in its host municipality. Category 46504 understates the extent of agrarian reform, because producers who have already obtained their title appear as owners. See Census tenure data and agrarian reform for a discussion.

For 2024, the Greenhouse Gas Emissions and Removals Estimation System (SEEG 13) estimated gross emissions of 588.5 thousand tCO2e (GWP-AR5) for the whole municipality, not only for family farming. Of this total, 6.4% came from land-use change and forestry and 78.0% from agriculture and livestock. Removals in the same year totalled 45.1 thousand tCO2e. Between the 2006 and 2017 Agricultural Censuses, the number of agricultural establishments in the municipality (family and non-family farms) rose by 6.7%, and their area fell by 43.5%.

Emissions and removals over time

The chart shows gross emissions and removals, along with emissions from land-use change and forestry and from agriculture and livestock, as estimated by SEEG 13 for the whole municipality from 1970 to 2024. Values are in thousand tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (thousand tCO2e, GWP-AR5). Removals are shown as absolute values. SEEG estimates land-use change and forestry emissions and removals only from 1990 onwards. Gross emissions therefore jump in 1990, because the series does not include this sector before that year. Hover over the chart (or tap it) to see the values for each year.
