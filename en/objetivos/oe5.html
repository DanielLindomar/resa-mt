SO5 · Innovation and social capital

Specific objective 5 of the RESA Project: innovation capacity, knowledge diffusion and social capital in the pilot areas. Objective, planned methods and implementation status
Preliminary version for the project team. Results may change as the study progresses. English translation under review. If the two versions differ, the Portuguese text prevails.

Objective

The fifth specific objective (SO5) is to understand how families in the pilot areas innovate, exchange knowledge and organise themselves. SO5 seeks to identify the factors that favour engagement and how families perceive the proposed changes. This is expected to make the diffusion of the Technology Package more effective and to strengthen the network linking families, researchers and public authorities.

Status as at 19 October 2026: planned
  • planned Participatory workshops in the pilot areas. To be held after the Phase 1 assessment; no confirmed date.
  • planned Workshop reports and community engagement protocols.

The badges are based on the project’s internal record and have yet to be validated by the project coordination team.

Planned methods

The planned method combines participatory workshops with semi-structured interviews. The workshops with farmers and community leaders are intended to validate the results of the assessment and to develop the recommendations jointly. The interviews with families and community leaders aim to capture perceptions and engagement factors. Practical training in the pilot areas is also planned, covering low-carbon management and access to specialised markets, such as those for carbon credits and payment for environmental services (PES). Finally, a dissemination plan is to include field days and plain-language materials such as booklets, short videos and simplified maps.

Expected results

The project proposal sets three targets for measuring progress on this objective. These are the degree to which communities engage with and take ownership of the Technology Package, the number of family farmers trained in low-carbon technologies, and the public policy proposals presented to decision-makers.

Ethical considerations

Activities with families are subject to approval by the Research Ethics Committee and to the free and informed consent of participants. In keeping with these safeguards, the website does not publish the names, photographs or individual data of families or participants, and workshop results are presented in aggregate form only.
