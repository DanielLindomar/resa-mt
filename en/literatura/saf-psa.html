AFS and PES in settlements

Dominicis et al. (2023) as the reference study for SO4, the 38 studies compared (identified by location, target group, arrangement and DOI) and the gaps identified in the literature
Preliminary version for the project team. The table only identifies each study (location, biome, target group, arrangement and DOI) and gives no numerical results. English translation under review. If the two versions differ, the Portuguese text prevails.

The reference study

The study by Dominicis et al. (2023), published in Forests (10.3390/f14102110), assesses the financial viability of agroforestry systems (AFS) established by family farmers, most of them agrarian-reform settlers, in the Descoberto River basin (Federal District, Cerrado). The authors compare results with and without payment for environmental services (PES), using indicators such as net present value (NPV) and internal rate of return (IRR).

The specific objective on bioeconomic scenarios (SO4) will replicate and extend this design in the project’s three pilot areas, one in each biome. The search for similar studies serves a dual purpose: to inform the choice of methods and to show what is still missing from the literature.

Gaps in the literature

Comparing the studies points to five gaps in the literature. The project treats them differently, depending on the status assigned to each: three are confirmed, one is partly filled and one is not asserted. The first two, both confirmed, concern geographical coverage. The search found no viability study of AFS or productive restoration in a settlement in Mato Grosso (gap 1), and the studies conducted in the state deal with smallholdings outside settlements, commercial farms or experiments. Nor did it find any such study in the Pantanal (gap 2), so the closest parameters come from the Cerrado of Mato Grosso do Sul, in the transition zone with the Pantanal.

Gap 3, on valuing carbon at market prices in settlements and small-scale production, is partly filled. Some studies include carbon in the cash flow of small producers, although most were conducted outside Brazil; in Brazil itself, cases are few and partial. By contrast, gap 4, on treating PES, carbon, risk and differences between families together in the same study, is confirmed, as no study combines all four elements. This combination is therefore SO4’s contribution.

Finally, gap 5, on land credit debt in the cash flow, is not asserted. The search did not target this point, which is relevant to the N. Sra. Aparecida case (land credit). A dedicated search is therefore needed before this gap can be asserted.
